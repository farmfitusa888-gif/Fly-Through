# Sales Assets

Everything here is written to be used verbatim. Nothing claims a result we cannot
evidence — see `business/99-sources.md` for which statistics are safe to cite and
which are not.

## 1. Cold email — the free sample offer

**Subject:** `your listing at {address}`

> Hi {name} — saw your listing at {address}. Nice photos.
>
> I turn listing photos into 40-second drone-style videos. No shoot, no pilot, no
> second visit — I just use the photos you already have and send it back the next
> day.
>
> Want me to do that one free so you can see it? If you like it, it's $249 a
> listing after that. If you don't, keep it anyway.
>
> Just reply with a link to the listing and I'll take the photos from there.
>
> — {you}
> {portfolio link}

**Why it is built this way.** Short enough to read on a phone. Names a specific
listing, so it cannot read as a blast. Leads with the objection (no shoot, no
pilot) rather than a feature. States the price up front, because hiding it wastes
both parties' time. Asks for a link, not an upload — the lowest-friction reply
possible.

**Do not** add statistics to this email. It converts on specificity and low
friction, not persuasion.

### Follow-up, 4 days later
> Hi {name} — still happy to make that video of {address} free, no strings.
> Takes me a day. Want it?

One follow-up. Then stop.

## 3. Photographer partner pitch — the highest-leverage conversation

**Subject:** `adding video to your packages without buying a drone`

> Hi {name} — you're already shooting {N} listings a month. I can turn the photos
> you've already delivered into a 40-second drone-style listing video, next day.
>
> You'd sell it at $349 as your own product and pay me $199. No new gear, no
> pilot's licence, no second site visit, nothing changes about how you shoot.
>
> It also ships with the AI disclosure agents now need — burned into the video,
> plus a hosted page with the untouched originals, which is what California's new
> rule actually asks for. Most AI video tools don't do that and it lands on the
> agent.
>
> Want me to run one of your recent shoots through it free so you can see the
> output?

**Why this matters more than any other asset here:** one photographer partner is
worth roughly thirty cold agents. If you only send one email this week, send this
one.

## 4. Objection handling

**"Is this actually a drone?"**
> No, and I'll never say it is. It's AI-generated camera motion built from your
> real photos — the video says so on screen, and it links to your untouched
> originals. That's deliberate: NAR Article 12 requires disclosing altered
> imagery, and in California AB 723 requires a link to the original. If you want
> real aerial footage, hire a licensed pilot — that's a different product and it
> costs more.

*Never soften this. The honest answer is the differentiator.*

**"Why not just hire a videographer?"**
> You should, on a listing that can carry $500 and a week. This is for the other
> ones — $249, next day, no scheduling, using photos you already paid for.

**"Will it look fake?"**
> Every shot starts and ends on your actual photos. The AI only fills the travel
> between two real frames, so it can't invent a room or a wing that isn't there.
> That's exactly why I'd rather show you than argue about it — send me a listing
> and judge the output.

**"$249 seems like a lot for AI."**
> A walkthrough video runs $300–$500 and a cinematic one $700–$1,200. This is
> cheaper and next-day. And you're not paying for compute, you're paying for a
> tour that's ordered like a showing and disclosed correctly so it can't come back
> on your licence.

**"I'll think about it."**
> Fair. Want the free one on your current listing while you do? Costs you a link.

**"Can you remove the disclosure card?"**
> No — and I'd push back on anyone who says yes. NAR Article 12 requires
> disclosing altered imagery and California fines run into the thousands. The card
> is 2.5 seconds and it's what makes the video safe for you to post. It's the
> reason to buy from me rather than a $19/month app.

*This objection converts more often than it loses. Do not bend on it.*

## 5. Delivery email — where the referral is earned

**Subject:** `{address} — your video`

> Hi {name} — here's the video for {address}.
>
> - **Landscape** (MLS, YouTube, your site): {link}
> - **Vertical** (Reels, TikTok, Stories): {link}
> - **Thumbnail**: {link}
> - **Your original photos**: {originals link}
>
> Paste this into your MLS remarks — it's the disclosure the video needs:
>
> > {mls_caption}
>
> Two things:
> 1. The video already has the AI disclosure on the front, so you're covered.
> 2. **Who else in your office is still posting listings with only photos?**
>    Happy to do one free for them the same way I did for you.
>
> — {you}

**Ask the referral question every single time.** Delivery is the only moment the
client is impressed. That is when the question is cheapest to ask and most likely
to be answered.

## 6. Retainer pitch — deliver on the second paid job

> You've bought two of these now. If you're doing 5+ listings a month, the
> retainer is $899 for five — $179 each instead of $249, and I hold a 24-hour slot
> for you so you don't have to ask.

Every second sale ends with this sentence. Retainers are what turn this from
freelancing into a business.
