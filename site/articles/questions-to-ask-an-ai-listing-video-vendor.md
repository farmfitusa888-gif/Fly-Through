AI video from listing photos went from novelty to commodity in about eighteen
months. The output ranges from genuinely good to actively damaging, and the
difference is not visible in a demo reel — which is the problem, because a demo
reel is what you get shown.

These are the five questions that separate the two. Four have a right answer.
The fifth is the one worth arguing about.

## 1. "Does the model see my property, or only my photographs?"

This is the question underneath every quality problem in the category.

Most AI video takes **one** photo and generates a flight around it. Everything
past the edge of that frame is invented: the model is asked what the rest of the
building looks like, and it answers confidently. Windows multiply. A second
storey appears. The back of the house is a house, just not yours.

The alternative anchors **both ends** of every shot to real photographs. The
model is never asked what the property looks like — it is told, twice, and asked
only how a camera gets between the two. It cannot invent the far end of the
shot, because the far end is a photograph you supplied.

**A good answer sounds like:** "every shot starts and ends on one of your
images."

**A bad answer sounds like:** "our model is very accurate."

Accuracy is a claim. Anchoring is a constraint, and constraints are checkable.

## 2. "Do you edit my photographs?"

It sounds like a question about taste. It is a question about whether your
disclosure works.

Under **California's §10140.8**, a digitally altered image in a property
advertisement needs a conspicuous statement *and* a link to the original,
unaltered image. That link points at a page holding your originals.

Now: if your vendor colour-corrects, crops or "enhances" your stills before
generating from them, the page they host contains *their* versions. You are
publishing a link that claims to show the unaltered original and does not. The
disclosure looks satisfied and isn't.

**The right answer is "no, never."** Not "only lightly." A vendor who does not
touch your files can host them and say truthfully that these are the images you
sent.

Worth knowing, because it cuts the other way too: ordinary editing you do
yourself is explicitly **outside** the statute. Lighting, sharpening, white
balance, colour correction, angle, straightening, cropping and exposure are all
excluded. [We wrote that up separately](/writing/ab-723-what-it-actually-requires/).

## 3. "What exactly do I get, besides the video?"

The video is the easy part. Ask what arrives with it.

A complete delivery is:

- the master, and a vertical cut if you post to Reels or TikTok
- an on-screen disclosure mark
- **a hosted page carrying your unaltered originals**, at a real URL
- a QR code pointing at that page, for print
- caption text you can paste into the MLS remarks

The third item is the one almost nobody ships, and it is the one the statute
actually asks for. A disclaimer in the caption satisfies the first duty and
ignores the second entirely.

If the answer is "we give you the file and a suggested disclaimer," you have
been sold the easy half and kept all of the liability.

## 4. "Will you make me a testimonial?"

Say yes to this and you have learned everything you need to know.

The **FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials**, in force since October
2024, prohibits AI-generated testimonials **regardless of disclosure**. You
cannot label your way out of it. Civil penalties run to tens of thousands of
dollars per violation.

A vendor who offers to generate a happy-buyer clip either does not know this or
does not care, and both answers tell you how much attention the rest of their
compliance thinking has had.

The right answer is a flat no, ideally with the reason attached.

## 5. "Who owns it, and for how long?"

The one without a single right answer — but there is a right *shape*.

You want the finished file outright, usable anywhere, indefinitely. Watch for:

- per-platform licences ("web only", "social only")
- annual renewals on media you already paid for
- files that stop working, or get taken down, if you stop subscribing
- the vendor reusing your property in their own marketing without asking

None of these are automatically unreasonable. All of them should be **stated
before** you buy rather than discovered in a renewal email eleven months later.

---

## The short version

| Ask | You want to hear |
|---|---|
| Does the model see the property or the photos? | Both ends of every shot are your images |
| Do you edit my photographs? | No, never |
| What arrives besides the video? | A hosted originals page, not just a disclaimer |
| Will you make me a testimonial? | No — and here's the rule |
| Who owns it? | You do, outright, stated up front |

None of these are hard questions. That is rather the point: a vendor who has
thought about any of this will answer all five in a minute, and one who has not
will need to get back to you.
