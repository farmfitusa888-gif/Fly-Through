# V3 — what would make this the best in its class

V1 is a service that renders anchored films and ships them compliantly. V2 is
that service selling itself: upload, pay, receive, re-order, refer.

V3 is the question neither answers: **why would someone choose this over the
next tool that can also make a video from a photo?** Model quality is not the
answer — it converges, and buying it is a subscription anyone can take out. The
durable answers are all in the same place: things that get better because *this*
system has run *these* jobs, and that a competitor with a better model still
would not have.

Ranked by defensibility per unit of effort, not by how interesting they are.

---

## 1. Learn from what actually sold

**The idea.** Every delivered film has an outcome attached to it that we
currently throw away. A listing goes under contract in nine days or ninety. A
VIN sells or sits. Ask one question thirty days after delivery — *did it sell?*
— and store the answer against the brief that produced the film.

**Why it is the strongest thing on this list.** After two hundred jobs you can
say, with evidence nobody else has: *listings that open on the kitchen go under
contract faster than listings that open on the exterior, in this price band, in
this market.* That is not a feature. It is a reason the tenth customer gets a
better film than the first, and it is the only asset here a competitor cannot
buy, licence or copy.

**What it needs.** One nullable outcome column, one scheduled email, one report.
The infrastructure is already there — `events` is append-only and every brief is
already stored as JSON.

**The trap.** Do not let it become a dashboard. Nobody buys a dashboard. It
should surface as one sentence at brief time: *"Kitchen-first tours in this
price band go under contract about eleven days sooner. Want us to lead on the
kitchen?"*

---

## 2. Shoot-time capture, not upload-time regret

**The idea.** A phone page that shows the shot list for the vertical, uses the
live camera, and tells the photographer *while they are standing there* that the
aerial needs to be over the same side as the last ground shot.

**Why.** Every quality problem this project has hit traces to the source set:
the "different house" aerial, mixed white balance across anchor pairs, a missing
bridge shot between the front and back of a property. The shot guides exist and
are good. A guide read the night before is worth a fraction of a check performed
at the moment of capture, and the difference is a re-shoot the customer cannot
do once they have left.

**What it needs.** The taxonomies and `viewpoint.assess` already encode every
rule. This is a camera view, a checklist, and running the check that already
exists against frames as they are taken.

**The trap.** Do not build a camera app. Build a web page that opens the camera.
Nobody installs an app to sell one house.

---

## 3. Ship the film to where it is used

**The idea.** Delivery is a download today. It should be a push: straight into
the MLS media slot, the dealer's DMS, the Shopify product gallery, the scheduled
Reel.

**Why.** The gap between "the film is ready" and "the film is live" is where
value quietly leaks. An agent who has to download, re-upload and write a caption
does it for the listings they care about and skips the rest — so the retainer
under-delivers for reasons that have nothing to do with the films.

**What it needs.** One integration at a time, starting with whichever the first
ten customers actually use. Never build the second before the first has been
used by a real customer for a month.

**The trap.** Integration work is endless and looks productive. Cap it: an
integration that has not been used in sixty days gets deleted.

---

## 4. Sell the compliance layer on its own

**The idea.** The originals page, the QR, the caption text, the audit trail —
unbundled, priced per listing, for people who make their AI video somewhere
else.

**Why.** It is already built, already researched, already the hardest part to
copy, and it is the only part of this business a competitor cannot ship in a
weekend. It also reaches the segment that will never buy the video: brokerages
who already have a media vendor and a compliance problem.

**The trap.** It cannibalises nothing, but it *does* legitimise competitors.
Only worth doing once the film side has a real moat of its own — which is item 1.

---

## 5. Two more verticals the taxonomy already fits

The architecture is taxonomy-as-data: seven of ten pipeline modules port
unchanged. Adding a vertical is a taxonomy, a phrase bank and a compliance
basis, not a rewrite.

- **Land and acreage.** The standing hypothesis in `07-open-questions.md`, and
  the place the relative advantage is largest: real drones are logistically
  hardest over acreage, so a synthetic flight is worth most there.
- **Rental and hospitality.** Same room taxonomy as residential, a different
  compliance basis, and a customer who re-orders every time the listing changes
  rather than once per sale.

**The trap.** Both are Phase-3-and-later work. Adding a vertical before the
first is at $5k/month is how a business ends up with four half-sold products.

---

## What V3 is deliberately not

**A better model.** It converges, and it is a subscription anyone can buy.
Anchoring is the moat, and it works precisely because it does *not* depend on
the model being good at inventing things.

**True 3D / Gaussian splatting.** A better product with a far longer path to
revenue. It also throws away the compliance position: a splat is not a
photograph, and the originals page stops meaning anything.

**Virtual staging.** Lucrative, adjacent, and fatal. The moment we edit a
client's photograph, the originals page stops containing originals and the
entire wedge collapses. Declining it is a strategic choice, not an oversight.

**An AI UGC / testimonial product.** The FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and
Testimonials bans AI-generated testimonials outright, regardless of disclosure.
There is no version of this worth building.

---

## The order

1 first, because it compounds and nothing else on the list does. 2 next, because
it fixes the input problem that limits quality on every job. 3 when a customer
asks for it by name. 4 and 5 only after Phase 3 in the roadmap passes.
